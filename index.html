Privacy

Privacy

Your calendar content stays in Chrome storage, with optional sync across your devices. Anonymous extension usage analytics are off unless you explicitly allow four minimal signals, and they never include calendar content, notes, URLs, account identity, or shared-link contents. YearMap does not sell personal data, and ICS export files are generated locally in your browser.

FAQ

Can I use YearMap on my phone?

Creating and editing plans requires the extension in Chrome on a computer. Chrome on phones does not run the extension. You can open the read-only sample and shared calendar links in your phone’s browser.

Does it replace my new tab?

Yes. YearMap opens when you create a new Chrome tab while the extension is enabled. You can also open the calendar in a regular tab from the extension icon.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

YearMap is free and requires no account. You can organize plans with up to five color categories.

Does it connect to Google or Apple Calendar?

You can export your ranges as an ICS file for Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or another calendar app. Export is a file download, not a live calendar connection.

Where is my calendar stored?

Your calendar content stays in Chrome storage, with optional sync across your devices. Extension usage analytics are off unless you choose to allow them.

What can someone see through a shared link?

A shared link opens a read-only snapshot of your calendar. It does not update when you change your plans. Anyone with the link can view the snapshot, so share it only with people you intend to see it.

Can I preview it before installing?

Yes. Open the sample calendar to explore a filled-in, read-only year view. Creating and editing your own plans requires the Chrome extension.
